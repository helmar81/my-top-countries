Morocco's culinary scene is a vibrant mix of flavors, spices, and centuries-old traditions. Among its many iconic dishes, **Couscous** stands out as a true cultural staple. 

## The Tradition of Friday Couscous

In Morocco, couscous is much more than just a meal; it is a weekly ritual that brings families and communities together. Traditionally, couscous is served every **Friday** after the midday Jumu'ah prayers. Families gather around a large communal platter to share this hearty dish, which is typically piled high with slowly simmered vegetables, tender meat (such as lamb or chicken), and aromatic broth. 

### Why Friday?
Friday is the holiest day of the week in Islam, and preparing couscous is a labor-intensive process that honors the significance of the day. The preparation involves steaming the semolina granules multiple times over a simmering stew, ensuring they are perfectly light, fluffy, and infused with the flavors of the broth below. 

When you visit Morocco, make sure to experience a traditional Friday couscous—it's the perfect introduction to the warmth and generosity of Moroccan hospitality!
